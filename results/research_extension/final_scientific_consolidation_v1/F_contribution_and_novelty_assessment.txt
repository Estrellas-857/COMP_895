SCIENTIFIC CONTRIBUTION AND NOVELTY ASSESSMENT

Overall characterization
This is a systematic empirical study with a reproducible evaluation and engineering component. Its strongest claim is a model- and dataset-specific account of the difference between probability quality and selective reliability under adverse-condition shift. It is not a new segmentation architecture, calibration algorithm or risk-guaranteed selective classifier. The paired experiment provides controlled evidence about calibration transfer, with limited sequence support.

A. Established method implementation
SegFormer-B0 supplies the segmentation model. MSP and entropy provide familiar confidence summaries. Positive scalar temperature scaling optimizes NLL without changing canonical labels. A fixed uncertainty threshold produces an accept/reject mask. None of these operations is a new algorithm. The Mix objective fixes equal domain weights within the same scalar calibration family and introduces no new learned inference mechanism.
Primary anchors checked for this review:
- SegFormer, Xie et al., NeurIPS 2021: https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2021/hash/64f1f27bf1b4ec22924fd0acb550c235-Abstract.html
- MSP error detection, Hendrycks and Gimpel, ICLR 2017: https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02136
- Temperature scaling, Guo et al., ICML 2017: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html
- Selective classification, Geifman and El-Yaniv, 2017: https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08500
These establish prior methods. The selective-classification reference contains a different risk-control methodology; its guarantees do not transfer to this project's coverage-quantile threshold.

B. Experimental and engineering contributions
The project records condition-specific baselines, separates 802 calibration-fitting images from 798 threshold-selection images, fixes global parameters before final operating-point evaluation, and exposes an RGB-only inference interface. Source/artifact hashes, dataset-access audits, prediction-invariance checks, operating-count parity, cache provenance and explicit metric denominators make the empirical record inspectable. A measured baseline-versus-final runtime comparison quantifies the cost of the measured preprocessing-to-CPU-output region, excluding disk decoding, model loading and serialization. These are legitimate reproducibility and engineering contributions, although integrity checks do not themselves prove generalization, statistical independence or deployment safety.

The controlled transfer design is especially useful: NightPair and NormalPair use the same 101 matched scene population, so their comparison does not confound domain choice with a Night-203 versus Normal-101 fitting count. The larger original global fit remains a contextual reference. Prior validation use and possible recording-family dependence prevent treating the extension as an untouched independent benchmark.

C. Empirical research contributions
1. Condition-dependent probability behavior. NLL improves across all original adverse conditions, while ECE and error ranking respond differently. This is more informative than reporting a single average calibration score because it exposes a negative result in Fog/Rain and for calibrated entropy.
2. Separation of calibration and selective ranking. On matched Night validation, ECE falls from 14.5885% to 6.3518% and NLL from 1.34652 to 1.25852 with NightPair versus global, while risk at 80% coverage slightly increases. The direction repeats across the two available sequence IDs. This is the clearest empirical contribution.
3. A controlled Normal–Night transfer trade-off. Domain-specific temperatures favor different domains. The predefined Mix improves average-domain NLL modestly without useful ranking gains. This supports a practical calibration compromise, with no claim that a new algorithm has been invented.
4. Semantic failure diagnosis. True-Sky errors often carry Building/Vegetation predictions, so a predicted-Sky rejection rule would miss the relevant failure population. Supplemental ambiguity strata show much higher observed risk in uncertain regions, while retaining the primary semantic-valid mask. These analyses explain limitations that global averages conceal.
The broad proposition that uncertainty can fail under distribution shift is established prior work, for example Ovadia et al., NeurIPS 2019: https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/2019/hash/8558cb408c1d76621371888657d2eb1d-Abstract.html . The project's value is the specific audited evidence and controlled comparison. This targeted reference check is not an exhaustive novelty search and cannot establish a "first" claim.

D. Potential future methodological contributions
Condition-aware rejection would require a deployable condition signal and validation on independent recording groups. Class-aware risk allocation would require stable predicted-class error behavior and sufficient support in separate selection/evaluation populations. Semantic-confusion-aware uncertainty would need features that identify wrong labels despite high softmax confidence. Improved confident-error detection would need comparative evidence against the present scores at matched coverage. None was implemented or evaluated here. These are motivated research questions, not completed contributions or approved next experiments.

Academic value
The work is appropriate for a graduate research course because it formulates separable questions, implements and verifies established methods, controls parameter-selection populations, studies multiple conditions, interprets negative results, and critically audits the limits of its own metrics. A new network is not required for empirical research value. The instructor can assess whether the experimental design and interpretation answer the questions, rather than whether every metric improves.

For a stronger peer-reviewed paper
A stronger general claim would usually require clearer positioning against recent task-specific calibration/selective-segmentation literature, independent recording-level evaluation, broader model or dataset support, and an uncertainty analysis that respects recording dependence. A methodological paper would additionally require a concrete new method and suitable controls. These are conditional requirements for a different paper scope, not required extensions to the completed course project. Narrowing the current claims is preferable to reopening experiments now.

Candidate contribution paragraph
We present a systematic empirical investigation of confidence calibration and selective semantic-segmentation reliability for a fixed Cityscapes-fine-tuned SegFormer-B0 model on ACDC. We evaluate condition-specific segmentation and error detection, implement an RGB-only rejection pipeline with separately fitted and frozen calibration and selection parameters, and study calibration transfer using matched Normal–Night training scenes. The results show that improvements in multiclass probability quality and top-label calibration do not consistently improve error ranking or risk at comparable coverage. A controlled paired analysis identifies a Normal–Night calibration trade-off and persistent highly confident semantic errors, defining the practical limits of the evaluated scalar calibration and rejection mechanisms.

Recommended Introduction/Conclusion treatment
State the empirical questions and controlled design in the Introduction. Use the Conclusion to report the observed separation between probability quality and ranking, the cost in coverage, and the restricted generalization scope. Avoid "novel framework", "robust autonomous driving system", "safety guarantee", "state of the art", or "first demonstration".
