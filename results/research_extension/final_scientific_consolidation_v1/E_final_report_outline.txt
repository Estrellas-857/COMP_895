Working title
Confidence Calibration and Selective Semantic Segmentation under Adverse Driving Conditions: An Empirical Study on ACDC

Purpose and organizing principle
This is a writing plan, not the paper, a new experimental proposal, or a claim of methodological novelty. Organize the report around the scientific argument: segmentation errors motivate uncertainty scores; imperfect confidence motivates calibration; calibration requires a separate evaluation of ordering and rejection; matched Normal–Night transfer exposes where probability improvements fail to improve selective ranking. Use historical stage names only as provenance labels. Avoid one independent chapter for every script or substage.

The main results should answer RQ1–RQ5 from C_research_questions_and_evidence_matrix.txt. Give the largest share of results space to global calibration, the frozen risk–coverage trade-off, and the controlled transfer comparison. Present selected semantic failures and computation as evidence that limits the interpretation. Retain negative results.

Population and metric conventions to enforce throughout
- Full adverse validation: 406 images, comprising Fog 100, Rain 100, Snow 100 and Night 106.
- Original paired baseline: 203 Normal–Adverse pairs, including 53 Normal–Night pairs. Its Normal and adverse sides use separate GT maps.
- Original global fitting: 802 adverse training images. Threshold selection: 798 different adverse training images. These are parameter-selection populations, not validation results.
- Controlled transfer fitting: 101 matched Normal–Night training pairs, or 202 images. Controlled transfer evaluation: 53 pairs, or 106 images, of which only 53 are Night. Never call this the full 106-image Night validation population.
- Report pooled pixel-weighted metrics separately from equal-image paired differences. State whether coverage includes all pixels or only semantic-valid pixels.
- Stage 2/3 ranking and Stage 5B risk–coverage use 1,000-bin summaries of all valid pixels. They are histogram approximations, not randomly sampled curves.
- Extension risk–coverage and AURC use shared weighted samples of 8,192 valid pixels per image. These are estimates. Exact full-pixel frozen-policy counts remain a separate result.
- Stage 5B has no reported AURC. Do not manufacture a comparable AURC by connecting unobserved low-coverage regions of its histograms.
- ECE is an absolute binwise gap; signed confidence-minus-accuracy is a different statistic. AP-style AUPRC is not trapezoidal PR area.

4. METHODS AND EVALUATION DEFINITIONS
Scientific purpose: define one common framework so results chapters can focus on findings rather than repeat formulas.
Recommended subsections and required content:
4.1 Canonical segmentation and uncertainty. Prediction is argmax of unscaled full-resolution logits. MSP uncertainty is 1 - max softmax(z/T); entropy is -sum(p log(max(p, 1e-12))) / log(19).
4.2 Calibration. Define multiclass NLL, 15-bin top-label ECE and signed confidence-minus-accuracy. Explain global T, NightPair, NormalPair and the fixed equal-domain Mix objective. Fitting uses all valid training pixels and safeguarded Newton optimization in beta = 1/T; no validation tuning occurs.
4.3 Selective prediction. Accept uncertainty <= tau. The exact 80th-percentile threshold is fitted on all image pixels of the 798 reserved training images, without labels. GT is used only afterward for diagnostics. Define valid coverage, all-pixel coverage and accepted error separately. Rejection never changes the stored semantic prediction.
4.4 Ranking evaluation. Incorrect pixels are positive for AUROC/AP. Describe histogram ROC integration and AP-style sum(delta recall * precision). For Stage 5B, define whole-bin acceptance, nearest achievable coverage and the absence of AURC. For the extension, define shared samples, N_valid/n_sample weights, label-independent tie keys, nearest weighted coverage and right-step estimated AURC. Do not call these interchangeable estimators.
4.5 Aggregation and conditional errors. Distinguish pooled counts from equal-image paired differences. Define confidence >= 0.90, error rate within the high-confidence group, and the fraction of all errors that are highly confident. Define predicted-class and GT-class groups separately.
4.6 Interpretation rules. Compare selective risk at comparable actual coverage. Fixed-tau results after changing T are cutoff-transfer diagnostics. The practical-interest criterion is one percentage point at approximately 80% coverage; it is not a significance threshold.

10. DISCUSSION, LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE DIRECTIONS
Scientific purpose: synthesize the evidence across questions rather than repeat every numerical table.
Recommended discussion order:
10.1 What changed: probability quality and confidence assignments, while canonical segmentation stayed fixed.
10.2 What did not follow: no consistent practically useful selective-ranking gain from the strongest paired calibration improvement.
10.3 What rejection achieved: a lower-error accepted subset at a coverage cost, with persistent confidently wrong semantics.
10.4 What the study contributes: systematic empirical findings, matched-population transfer evidence and auditable engineering.
Required limitations: one backbone and one dataset; prior exploratory validation use; only 53 paired validation scenes and two inherited sequence IDs; possible dependence among recording chapters; original train/validation sequence overlap; scene correspondence without pixel alignment; semantic-ignore versus ambiguity masks; pooled class imbalance; histogram/sampling and precision/tie limitations; no downstream safety test or combined-weather benchmark.
Interpretation of negative results: failure to meet the practical-interest criterion is sufficient to recommend stopping added class-aware complexity for this project. It is not proof that no calibrated method or semantic-aware score could ever help. Do not inflate a small balanced-NLL improvement into a new method contribution, and do not minimize the empirical value of a controlled negative result.
