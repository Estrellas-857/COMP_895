EXECUTIVE SCIENTIFIC SUMMARY
Uncertainty-Aware Semantic Segmentation under Adverse Driving Conditions

The completed project supports a coherent empirical study of a fixed segmentation model under distribution shift. Its strongest finding is that probability quality, calibration error, error ranking, and accepted-pixel risk can change differently. Substantial calibration improvements do not establish better semantic predictions or practically better selective ranking. This is a qualified result for the evaluated model and populations, rather than a universal theorem about calibration.

The study uses Cityscapes-fine-tuned SegFormer-B0 without segmentation retraining. The original validation population contains 406 adverse images: 100 each for Fog, Rain and Snow, and 106 for Night. An original 802-image training subset fits one temperature, while 798 different training images select a rejection threshold. The extension controls its fitting population using 101 matched Normal–Night training pairs and evaluates all 53 annotated validation pairs against each image's own labels. The Normal and Night views correspond at scene level and are not pixel aligned.

Baseline performance establishes the reliability problem. Full Night validation has 22.378% union-defined mIoU and 60.262% pixel accuracy, compared with Fog's 63.793% and 91.938%. On the matched 53-pair population, Normal and Night mIoU are 50.368% and 22.372%. These paired observations reduce some scene-population mismatch but do not isolate illumination causally. Class imbalance and persistent semantic confusions remain relevant even when pixel accuracy appears high.

MSP and normalized entropy provide useful error scores, with all original condition-level AUROCs above 0.77. Night MSP AUROC/AP are 0.77324/0.63998, against an error prevalence of 0.39738. The comparatively high Night AP should not be read as superior detection: AP depends on prevalence, while Night has the lowest AUROC. In full Night validation, 47.162% of all raw prediction errors have confidence at least 0.90. This differs from the 27.747% error rate within the high-confidence group. Selected failure cases illustrate semantic errors that scalar confidence can miss.

Global temperature scaling, T=2.124186626456894, lowers NLL in all four original conditions. It lowers ECE in Snow and Night but raises it in Fog and Rain. Error-ranking responses also differ: calibrated MSP improves Night AUROC/AP slightly, while calibrated entropy worsens both across all four conditions. The result therefore supports condition- and metric-dependent benefits, rather than the claim that calibration never helps error detection.

The RGB-only selective pipeline accepts pixels when 1−max softmax(z/T) is at most 0.386413580715649. This threshold achieves 80% all-image-pixel coverage on its reserved training population. It does not enforce 80% valid-pixel coverage on validation or every condition. On all 406 validation images, baseline error is 17.805%, while accepted error is 9.839% at 81.433% valid coverage. Full Night retains 68.671% valid coverage with 28.119% accepted error, compared with 39.738% unrejected error. Rejection removes predictions from the accepted set; it does not correct their semantic labels.

The paired extension makes the calibration/ranking distinction particularly clear. NightPair, T=2.807826, improves Night NLL/ECE over global from 1.34652/14.5885% to 1.25852/6.3518%. At approximately 80% valid coverage, estimated Night risk instead rises from 32.7398% to 32.8926%. Both validation sequences show this direction. No tested change reaches the prespecified one-percentage-point practical improvement criterion. NormalPair, T=2.005173, has the lowest Normal NLL and ECE among the tested configurations. The predefined equal-domain Mix, T=2.496183, achieves balanced NLL 0.81650 versus global 0.84062, but improves only 0.00256 nats over NightPair and does not meet the prespecified practical ranking-gain criterion. Even NightPair retains 1.58 million wrong pixels among 25.12 million Night pixels with confidence at least 0.90.

The contribution combines a reproducible empirical evaluation, frozen selection and inference engineering, and a controlled matched-population transfer comparison. SegFormer, MSP, entropy, scalar temperature scaling and rejection are established methods. Mix is an application of scalar calibration to a predefined balanced objective. The legitimate research value lies in the quantified transfer trade-offs, negative ranking results, and analysis of confident semantic failures.

The main limits are one backbone, previously explored validation, only two paired Night sequence IDs, some original train/validation sequence-prefix overlap, class/pixel pooling, numerical tie effects, and approximate ranking evaluations. Stage 5B uses full-population score histograms; the extension samples valid pixels and estimates AURC. Exact frozen operating-point counts are separate. Recorded runtime compares preprocessing-to-CPU-output regions; disk decoding, model loading and serialization are excluded. It does not isolate temperature-scaling cost. A documented CUDA-setting correction restored historical parity without changing fits or scientific criteria.

The project is sufficiently complete for a graduate empirical research report. Proceed to report and presentation drafting after this consolidation is reviewed. No essential new experiment is needed for the narrowed claims. Emphasize condition-dependent calibration, the distinction between calibration and ranking, and the remaining semantic limits of rejection.
