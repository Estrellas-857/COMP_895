Review decision
The completed work supports a coherent graduate empirical study. The central conclusion is supported with qualification: substantial probability-calibration improvements do not consistently yield practically meaningful improvement in selective ranking in the evaluated setting. Global calibration does improve some original ranking metrics, so the report must avoid claiming that calibration never helps error detection. No essential new experiment is needed for the narrowed claims.

Three findings to emphasize
1. NLL, ECE, error ranking and accepted risk are different outcomes. On the same 53 Night validation images, global-to-NightPair ECE decreases from 14.5885% to 6.3518%, while estimated accepted risk near 80% coverage rises from 32.7398% to 32.8926%.
2. Calibration transfer is domain-dependent. NightPair improves Night probability metrics while worsening Normal; the predefined equal-domain Mix provides a modest balanced-NLL compromise without meeting the practical ranking-gain criterion.
3. Rejection lowers accepted error by giving up coverage and leaves confident semantic errors. Across full 406-image adverse validation, accepted risk is 9.8387% at 81.4332% valid coverage, versus 17.8054% unrejected error.
