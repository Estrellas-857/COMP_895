RESEARCH QUESTIONS
1. On Normal, global versus raw NLL decreases 0.110502 nats; ECE decreases 2.450671 percentage points. These two criteria are reported separately. GOPR0351: delta NLL=-0.095501; GOPR0356: delta NLL=-0.127237. NLL is lower on both available sequences.

2. On Night, NightPair versus NormalPair NLL decreases 0.121973 nats; ECE decreases 9.769391 percentage points. GOPR0351: delta NLL=-0.128476; GOPR0356: delta NLL=-0.114540. NLL is lower on both available sequences. This is descriptive consistency, not evidence from many independent sequences.

3. Changing NormalPair to NightPair gives delta NLL 0.046833 on Normal and -0.121973 on Night. The domains favor different temperatures by pooled NLL.

4. At requested 80% coverage on night, global versus raw: actual coverage 80.0001% versus 80.0000%; estimated accepted risk 32.7398% versus 32.7114% (delta +0.0284 percentage points). Interpret any actual-coverage mismatch alongside this risk difference. At requested 80% coverage on night, NightPair versus NormalPair: actual coverage 80.0001% versus 80.0000%; estimated accepted risk 32.8926% versus 32.7275% (delta +0.1651 percentage points). Interpret any actual-coverage mismatch alongside this risk difference. NLL/ECE changes measure calibration; ranking must be judged from the shared-sample curves and all coverage checkpoints. Fixed-threshold risk changes alone cannot establish better rejection.

5. Normal global-T: 1536260 wrong pixels among 76197600 pixels with confidence >=0.90; error rate within this group 2.0162%. 15.2920% of all errors have confidence >=0.90. Night global-T: 5305186 wrong pixels among 39864131 pixels with confidence >=0.90; error rate within this group 13.3082%. 13.1889% of all errors have confidence >=0.90.

6. The predefined equal-domain Mix has validation balanced NLL=0.816496; versus raw: delta balanced NLL=-0.533447; versus global: delta balanced NLL=-0.024119; versus NightPair: delta balanced NLL=-0.002562; versus NormalPair: delta balanced NLL=-0.040132. Inspect each domain and sequence in addition to this mean. No mixture weight was selected on validation; this is established scalar temperature scaling, not a new calibration algorithm.

7. This experiment does not test a learned class-specific rejection policy. Descriptive per-class errors cannot establish that extra thresholds would help. Stop at the present scalar/paired study unless a separate training-only analysis identifies stable predicted-class error differences with adequate sequence support and a predeclared evaluation. True-sky errors predicted as building or vegetation are not addressed by a predicted-sky threshold. No additional class policy is recommended solely from these previously explored validation results.

LIMITATIONS AND CONTRIBUTION
- Validation was previously explored.
- Only two Night validation sequence IDs are available; pixels are not independent observations.
- Corresponding Normal and Night images are not pixel aligned.
- Risk-coverage/AURC are shared-sample estimates, not full-population exact ranks.
- All 19 classes are descriptive; no class-specific policy was fitted.
The contribution is paired empirical evidence on scalar calibration transfer and rejection behavior. It does not establish a new calibration algorithm, causal illumination effects, performance on combined Night+Rain/Snow, or statistically independent evidence from individual pixels.
