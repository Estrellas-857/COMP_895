Post-hoc interpretation of frozen coefficients and predicted-class behavior

Scope and verification
All 34 stored pipelines were inspected without fitting or altering a model. Their
continuous input order is MSP, normalized entropy, and margin. LR+class appends
the 19 predicted-class one-hot coordinates in Cityscapes trainId order 0..18;
class 0 is road. Every classifier's positive outcome is segmentation error.
The intercept + continuous contribution + class contribution reconstructed the
stored decision function on every held-out QA pixel, maximum discrepancy
2.887e-15. Raw-coordinate algebra had maximum discrepancy 3.821e-6, attributable
to float32 StandardScaler/linear arithmetic versus float64 raw-coordinate algebra.

The tables use existing uniform valid-pixel samples with inverse inclusion
weights: 512/image for outer training summaries, 8192/image for held-out QA.
Class shares, error rates, mean scores and within-class metrics here are sampled
estimates, not new full-population primary results. All 19 classes are retained.
Small-class flags mean <100 sampled pixels, <20 errors, or <20 correct pixels;
they are descriptive flags, not statistical eligibility or significance tests.
High-confidence subsets MSP>=0.9 and MSP>=0.99 are exploratory diagnostics.

What a coefficient means
The class offsets are conditional log-odds contrasts at fixed continuous
features. They are not class error rates, causal effects, or standalone feature
importance. Centering subtracts the unweighted mean across all 19 category
coefficients and adds it to the intercept. A road-reference contrast subtracts
the road coefficient. Neither operation changes the fitted model.
MSP, entropy and margin are correlated and constrained by the same softmax
vector; the positive conditional MSP coefficient cannot be read as a claim
that increasing confidence generally increases error.

LR3 and LR+class also have different fitted continuous slopes and intercepts.
For the Night gopro_0376 outer fold, raw-unit slopes [MSP, entropy, margin] are
[19.2174, 16.3291, -8.1182] for LR3 and [20.4610, 16.1439, -8.9238] for LR+class.
For Fog gopro_0477 they are [20.3111, 16.1907, -8.6783] and
[19.9604, 15.3651, -8.7297]. It would therefore be incorrect to attribute the
entire difference between methods solely to class offsets. No counterfactual
model or counterfactual performance estimate has been constructed.

Night: observed class-reliability shift and cross-class ordering
In held-out gopro_0376, predicted vegetation occupies 16.74% of valid pixels.
Its estimated error rate is 14.17% in the corresponding outer training sample
but 58.46% in held-out QA. Mean LR3 score is 23.68%; mean LR+class score falls
to 20.61%. Its centered class offset is -0.2105, while road's is +0.4409:
the conditional vegetation-versus-road contrast is approximately -0.6514.

The high-confidence subset makes the transfer mismatch clearer. Predicted
vegetation with MSP>=0.99 occupies 5.924% of all valid held-out pixels; estimated
training error is 3.663%, held-out error 37.489%, and mean LR+class score 4.978%
(LR3 6.271%). Predicted road with MSP>=0.99 occupies 35.161% of all valid pixels;
its held-out error is only 3.741%, yet mean LR+class score is 8.555%. These are
subset means, not a statement that every vegetation pixel ranks below every
road pixel. They nevertheless provide direct descriptive evidence that the
learned class-conditioned score can assign lower average risk to a substantially
more error-prone held-out subset.

The vegetation shift also occurs in the other Night groups: estimated training
versus held-out error is 17.03% versus 40.75% for gopro_0351 and 16.01% versus
53.00% for gopro_0397. LR+class lowers mean vegetation score relative to LR3
in all three groups. Predicted buildings also become more error-prone than their
training averages, but their positive class offsets raise mean learned scores.
These observations are compatible with changing class reliability and feature
distributions under Night conditions; they do not establish a causal mechanism.

Within-class ranking changes are small for the dominant Night classes. In
gopro_0376, QA AUROC LR3 -> LR+class is road 0.9184 -> 0.9183, vegetation
0.7127 -> 0.7128, building 0.6998 -> 0.7001. In contrast, the existing full-pixel
overall group AUROC falls from 0.83179 to 0.76602. A class offset is constant
within a predicted class, so its direct ranking action is across classes; the
small observed within-class changes and large overall loss support cross-class
ordering as an important part of the failure. Refitted slopes and composition
also matter, and these diagnostics do not uniquely identify their contributions.

Sky is only about 0.62% of predicted valid pixels in Night gopro_0376; it should
not be assumed to explain the main failure merely because its offset is large.
Rare high-confidence sky subsets are flagged as small in the exported tables.

Fog: large low-error classes and selective cross-class reordering
In gopro_0477, gopro_0478 and gopro_0479, predicted sky occupies 36.50%, 38.70%
and 60.94% of valid pixels. Its held-out error is 1.99%, 1.53% and 1.30%, close
to corresponding training estimates 1.55%, 1.64% and 1.64%. Mean LR3 sky scores
17.53%, 15.90% and 15.57% become LR+class scores 2.81%, 2.43% and 2.50%, with
centered sky offsets around -2.20. Here a large low-error predicted category
retains approximately its training reliability, unlike Night vegetation.

Predicted buildings in the same Fog groups have held-out errors 74.52%, 62.17%
and 94.13%. Mean LR3 scores 28.73%, 28.46% and 38.56% rise to LR+class scores
35.74%, 37.69% and 49.39%. The learned scores still understate those empirical
error rates; improved ordering does not imply accurate probability calibration.
The simultaneous downweighting of a large reliable sky population and upweighting
of unreliable building predictions is consistent with the improved overall Fog
ranking and selective prediction observed in these groups.

Again, within-class ranking does not supply the main improvement. For gopro_0477,
QA sky AUROC is 0.8020 -> 0.8012 and building 0.8749 -> 0.8751 for LR3 -> LR+class,
while existing full-pixel overall AUROC rises 0.82104 -> 0.90703. The class-aware
model changes relative score levels across predicted classes rather than learning
new within-class spatial or visual features.

Fog benefits are not universal across every metric/group. Existing gopro_0475
full-pixel AUROC and AURC are slightly worse for LR+class than MSP despite better
risk at requested 80% coverage. Gopro_0480 does not improve risk80 over MSP.
This evidence supports a condition- and operating-point-dependent tradeoff,
not an unconditional claim that class features are superior.

Limits
The diagnostics are post-hoc and descriptive. Training/held-out class-rate shifts
can combine weather, scene composition and continuous-feature distribution
changes. Predicted classes are not true semantic classes. Sample pixels are
correlated; no pixel-IID tests or causal claims are made. There are only three
Night recording proxies, whose physical-session independence remains unproven.
No model, calibration, threshold, feature, or primary evaluation was changed.
