CSE 805 final presentation — minimal evidence plan

Suggested title
Learning to Predict Semantic Segmentation Errors Under Adverse Driving Conditions
Add the student's name on the title slide and the instructor-required slide footer.

One-sentence thesis
Class-aware learning improves a prespecified retrospective operating point, but
its ranking reliability depends on the condition: Fog improves most, while Night
exposes a severe low-coverage failure.

Seven-part research narrative

1. Problem and significance
   A frozen segmentation model can confidently assign the wrong semantic class.
   The task is to score which existing pixel predictions are likely to be wrong,
   so that accepting only a subset can reduce accepted error. This experiment
   measures pixel reliability; it does not demonstrate safer driving outcomes.

2. Baseline limitation
   MSP and entropy summarize the softmax distribution without an explicit
   predicted-category reliability term. Similar confidence can correspond to
   different error rates across predicted classes and conditions. These methods
   remain strong baselines: LR with confidence features alone brings little gain.

3. Our solution
   Train a regularized Logistic Regression error predictor using MSP, normalized
   entropy, margin, and predicted-class one-hot encoding. The backbone stays
   frozen, and ground truth defines training targets only. Compare MSP, entropy,
   LR3, and LR+class; the ablation identifies the value and cost of adding class.

4. Methodology
   Use 1,600 labeled images and 17 conservative recording proxies. Hold out one
   group at a time; tune within the remaining groups using grouped validation.
   Fit preprocessing only on the relevant training groups. Sample training pixels
   with documented weights, but evaluate all valid held-out pixels. Keep the
   separate 406 validation images out. Filename groups are not proven sessions.

5. Contribution and measured improvement
   The contribution is a student-trained, interpretable reliability model plus a
   reproducible grouped experiment and class-feature analysis. Mean accepted error
   near 80% coverage is 7.665% for LR+class versus 8.393% MSP and 8.351% entropy.
   Improvements occur in 14/17 and 15/17 groups, respectively. Fog benefits most.
   This demonstrates preprocessing, feature engineering, Logistic Regression,
   regularization, grouped validation, and performance evaluation from CSE805.

6. Negative result and explanation
   Mean AURC worsens despite the risk80 improvement. All three Night groups lose
   AUROC/AP/AURC. At common near-exact 10% QA coverage, LR+class has 12.51%, 26.45%,
   and 19.45% accepted error versus MSP's 1.49%, 0.67%, and 0.42%. Full-pixel counts
   show vegetation predictions dominate these errors, especially true sky/building
   misclassified as vegetation. Category offsets favor an unreliable held-out
   subset. This is within-group failure, not solely pooled-score comparability.

7. Limits and future work
   Retrospective80 is not a deployable coverage/error guarantee. Class-aware
   training-derived thresholds yield 65.65%–91.55% held-out coverage. Only three
   Night proxies, correlated pixels, residual session dependence, sampled
   diagnostics, and prior exploration limit generalization and causal claims.
   Verify session metadata and obtain independent Night data before revising the
   model; separately evaluate threshold transfer. No new model was trained in
   this interpretation pass, and no improved segmentation mIoU is claimed.

Closing wording
“Our contribution is a supervised error predictor and a controlled comparison,
not a new segmentation backbone. Predicted-class information improves selective
error at our prespecified retrospective operating point. The Night failures show
that this benefit does not imply better ranking everywhere or reliable future
coverage. Reporting both outcomes is part of the result.”
